DELE B1 vs DELE A2: differences and which one to choose

Updated in June 2026 · 9 min reading

Overview of both levels

The DELE A2 and DELE B1 are two consecutive levels of the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR), but the distance between them is greater than many candidates expect. Choosing the right level can save you time, money, and frustration.

The A2 corresponds to a basic user who can communicate in simple and routine situations. The B1, on the other hand, certifies an independent user capable of handling most situations that arise in everyday life, expressing opinions, and narrating experiences.

Differences in exam structure

Total duration

  • DELE A2: approximately 2 hours and 45 minutes
  • DELE B1: approximately 3 hours and 25 minutes (excluding the oral test)

Reading comprehension

In A2, the texts are brief and straightforward: advertisements, signs, menus, short emails. In B1, you face newspaper articles, longer informative texts, and formal correspondence. The difference is not only in length but also in syntactic and lexical complexity.

Listening comprehension

The audios of A2 are slow, with clear pauses and limited vocabulary. In B1, the speed approaches natural speech, speakers may have different accents, and the topics are more varied (news, interviews, phone conversations).

Written expression

A2 requires texts of 70-80 words on very specific topics (describing your home, telling about your routine). B1 demands texts of 100-150 words with argumentation, opinion, and more elaborate structure.

Oral expression

In A2, oral interaction is guided and predictable. In B1, you must be able to maintain a conversation, react to unexpected questions, and express your point of view with some fluency.

The jump in grammar and vocabulary

A2 Grammar

  • Present indicative (regular and irregular)
  • Basic perfect and simple past
  • Future with "ir a + infinitive"
  • Basic affirmative imperative
  • Simple comparatives and superlatives
  • Direct object pronouns

Additional B1 Grammar

  • Imperfect past and contrast with simple past
  • Simple future and conditional
  • Present subjunctive (wishes, opinions, evaluations)
  • Negative imperative
  • Conditional sentences (type 1 and 2)
  • Basic indirect style
  • Indirect object pronouns and combinations
  • Discourse connectors (however, therefore, although)

Vocabulary

A2 manages a vocabulary of about 1,500-2,000 words focused on immediate topics: family, shopping, transportation, food. B1 expands the repertoire to 3,000-4,000 words and includes topics such as health, environment, technology, education, and current affairs.

Which one do you need for Spanish nationality?

To apply for Spanish nationality, the official minimum requirement is level A2. However, there are important nuances:

  • The DELE A2 is sufficient to meet the legal requirement for language knowledge
  • In addition to DELE A2, you need to pass the CCSE test (Constitutional and Sociocultural Knowledge of Spain)
  • DELE B1 or higher exempts you from the CCSE test if obtained in a specific call that includes cultural content
  • Many lawyers recommend B1 because it demonstrates greater linguistic integration

If your only goal is nationality and your Spanish level is basic, A2 is the quickest option. If you already have an intermediate level or want a more useful certificate in the long term, B1 is a better investment.

When to skip A2 and go directly to B1?

Going directly to B1 without passing through A2 is perfectly possible and, in many cases, advisable. Consider going straight to B1 if:

  • You have been studying Spanish regularly for over a year
  • You can maintain everyday conversations without major difficulties
  • You understand the general idea of texts and audios on familiar topics
  • You know the past tenses (simple past, imperfect, perfect)
  • You live or have lived in a Spanish-speaking country

It makes no sense to spend time and money on A2 if your actual level is already close to B1. DELE A2 is not a prerequisite to take B1.

Necessary preparation time

For DELE A2

Starting from scratch, approximately 200-250 hours of study are needed to reach level A2. If you already have a foundation (for example, you have completed an A1 level course), you can prepare for the exam specifically in 2-3 months with 1-2 hours of daily study.

For DELE B1

From scratch, B1 requires about 400-500 accumulated hours. If you already have a solid A2 level, you will need an additional 150 to 200 hours to reach B1. This translates to 3-4 months of intensive preparation (2-3 hours daily) or 5-6 months at a moderate pace (1-1.5 hours daily).

Factors that accelerate preparation

  • Your native language (speakers of Portuguese, Italian, or French progress faster)
  • Language immersion (living in a Spanish-speaking environment)
  • Previous experience with other languages
  • Regular practice with native speakers
  • Use of specific materials for the DELE format

Cost and logistics

The price of DELE A2 in Spain is around €112 and that of B1 about €130 (prices 2026). The cost difference is minimal, reinforcing the idea that if your level allows, it is more efficient to go directly to B1.

Both exams are offered in the same calls and examination centers. You can register at any authorized center of the Instituto Cervantes worldwide.

Our recommendation

If you are unsure between A2 and B1, ask yourself these questions:

  1. Can I understand a simple newspaper article in Spanish? → If yes, aim for B1
  2. Can I write an email of more than 100 words expressing my opinion? → If yes, B1
  3. Do I understand movies or series in Spanish with Spanish subtitles? → If yes, B1
  4. Is my main goal just nationality and am I in a hurry? → Maybe A2 is more practical

In case of doubt, take a level test or try a B1 exam model. If you score more than 50% in reading and listening comprehension, you have a sufficient foundation to prepare for B1 in a few months.
